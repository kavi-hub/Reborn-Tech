# Reborn Tech — Source Review Notes

## Current Bulk GSM public positioning

Source reviewed: [Bulk GSM ITAD Services](https://bulkgsm.com/), 19 August 2026.

- The current public message is **“Secure, Compliant & Value-Driven IT Asset Disposition (ITAD)”** and frames the proposition as being beyond recycling: maximise asset value, protect data and ensure compliance.
- The visible site structure includes an ITAD process, proprietary data-wiping software and contact/request-assessment routes.
- Bulk GSM publicly claims its own proprietary data-security software, more than 20 years of experience, and a top 0.002% position among ITAD providers. These claims must be substantiated and formally approved before being repeated on Reborn Tech.

## Supplied Corporate Recycling Flow Process

The supplied process document describes a five-stage corporate workflow:

| Stage | Verified operational activity | Customer-facing evidence / outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Contact | Customer provides inventory and specifies equipment for recycling; a service agreement follows. | Defined brief and service scope. |
| 2. Collection | Nationwide secure collection for business; transfer-of-custody signed on collection. | Secure custody record. |
| 3. Inventory & Data Wipe | Equipment recorded in full detail in an asset-sustainability report; full data erasure report issued with destruction certification where provided. | Asset report and data-erasure evidence. |
| 4. Valuation | Equipment valuation agreed. | Value recovery decision. |
| 5. Payment | Bank details confirmed and payment proceeds. | Financial settlement. |

The document also displays Environment Agency registered waste carrier status, ADISA Erasure Engine v3 certification branding and Securaze Silver Partner branding. The exact current certification and permission to use each logo must be checked before use on Reborn Tech.

## Initial strategic implication

The website should put **secure collection, auditable inventory, verified data disposition and value recovery** at the centre of its public ITAD proposition. Repair belongs in the post-triage reuse pathway, alongside recycling and redistribution, rather than leading the message.

## Supplied Second Life and StayWell material

The Second Life document proposes a donation route in which surplus and retired equipment is collected, securely erased, refurbished or recycled, and then allocated to schools, non-profits and community programmes. It identifies documentation, asset counts and optional impact summaries as outputs relevant to CSR and ESG reporting.

The supplied **Recycle to Stay Well** campaign material positions the recovered value of redundant laptops, phones and IT equipment as a potential source of employee wellbeing, workplace engagement and community impact. It promises a free IT value assessment and uses the phrase **“Recycle your IT. Invest in your people.”**

StayWell’s own public website, [itsmental.co.uk](https://www.itsmental.co.uk/), presents wellbeing solutions for businesses and individuals, including Stay Well Approved, Stay Well Collective, the Stay Well App, the PERMA+V model, wellbeing talks, events and community programmes. This supports a general description of StayWell as a wellbeing-focused partner, but the exact commercial arrangement, donation flow, naming and claims require written approval before publication.

### Care with impact claims

The Second Life material includes non-specific statements such as “thousands of devices” and “significant volumes” redirected or diverted. These must **not** appear on the Reborn Tech site unless Reborn can provide a dated, defensible calculation and a reporting method. The site should use a clear impact-reporting framework rather than unsupported aggregate figures.

## Future customer portal requirements surfaced by the site-visit questionnaire

The questionnaire establishes the eventual digital information model for customer onboarding and role-based access:

| Information area | Examples in the current questionnaire | Likely future portal capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organisation and contacts | Primary and emergency contacts, addresses, on-site instructions | Account and site records with permissions. |
| Access and logistics | Operating hours, security protocols, loading, parking, escorting, PPE and staging space | Secure collection brief and job checklist. |
| Asset scope | Asset types, inventory availability, special-handling instructions | Inventory upload, asset categories and job scoping. |
| Data requirements | Required standards, on-/off-site wiping, handling requirements | Compliance preference and certificate request configuration. |
| Documentation | Destruction certificates, serial/asset-tag reports, post-disposal audits | Certificate repository and job-report export. |
| Commercial and scheduling | Timing, collection restrictions, custom services, valuation / remarketing | Request tracking, collection scheduling and outcome status. |

The future portal should be envisaged as **customer organisation > sites > collections/jobs > asset records > evidence/certificates > outcomes/impact reports**, with roles such as customer administrator, customer site contact, Reborn operations, Reborn compliance and Reborn administrator. It should not be built or described as live in the initial public release.

## Approved update: legacy archive and Securaze partner language

On 19 August 2026, the existing source repository was preserved as the **Reborn Handset Repair Website**. The legacy project at [`kavi-hub/reborn-landing-revive`](https://github.com/kavi-hub/reborn-landing-revive) has a dated `reborn-handset-repair-website-2026-08-19` tag and is archived on GitHub. This preserves the old repair-led identity without carrying it into the new ITAD site.

Kavi has confirmed that **Bulk GSM is a Securaze UK partner**. This can be used as a visible proof point on the new Reborn Tech site. Securaze’s official product information supports focused wording around data erasure, device diagnostics, asset management and tamper-proof erasure certificates.[1][2][3] The new public copy must therefore use wording such as “Data erasure powered by Securaze” or “Bulk GSM: Securaze UK partner” only where operationally accurate. It should not assert unverified licence, certification or standard-specific status beyond the current approved evidence.
